Reading, with CIs:
- **hm:** the deficit at |H| = 3 (−0.050) and at 13–24 (−0.104) have non-overlapping CIs — the gap **doubles with history length**. Since the dot model *improves* with |H| while fU is flat-to-falling, the extra ~0.05 is not "information the mean loses" (the dot mean has it) but the prototype layer's inability to read a longer mean — the **H_geo** signature, on the composition side (the layer is frozen here, so the only lever is how q_u is shaped before it enters the cosine). fU-noid ≡ fU within CI in every band: on hm the ID row is inert (P5 absorbs nothing because there is nothing to absorb beyond the mean).

So for the longest histories the layer costs nothing extra — the loss there is the mean composition itself, shared by both columns (**H_comp**-type; the plain dot cannot recover it either). Nonlinear composition, if it helps anywhere on ml-1m, must help *both* columns in the long-history bands (that is what the 2×2 tests). fU-noid is 0.01–0.02 below fU in the mid bands here — the ID row does carry something on ml-1m (56-film means are far more informative to memorise against).

**G1 clause 1 verdict: the fU-vs-dot gap is NOT flat across |H| on either dataset** (hm: monotone growth; ml-1m: peaked, vanishing at the tail). The plan's "if flat AND tuple-ID ≈ fI-noid → both levers weak" branch is not taken; the user-side 2×2 is live.

What this establishes for the programme:
1. **The unit test holds:** on every dot model the fixed-game Shapley equals the linear per-purchase share to float32 precision (the game is additive). The instrument is exact where it must be.
2. **Under the frozen cosine layer, "exact-linear shares" and exact Shapley are different attributions.** Rank agreement stays high (Spearman 0.93–0.96) but the *most important purchase* differs for **1 in 4 hm users and ~1 in 2 ml-1m users**, and the L1 distance between the two attributions is 44–63 % of the attributed mass on hm (> 100 % on ml-1m). The linear split is exact *given the full-history normaliser ‖q_H‖*; the game-theoretic view, which lets ‖q‖ move with the coalition, disagrees on magnitudes. This is the concrete content of caveat (b)'s "cosine is a tolerated nonlinearity" and must become the regime-1 disclosure in the generalised Score Fidelity (plan §7): *exact given ‖q‖*, with the Shapley disagreement as its measured size. It also means the plan's SP3 faithfulness battery has a non-trivial baseline: even today's linear model does not agree with Shapley about its own top purchase under the cosine.
3. **The prototype layer makes recommendations insensitive to any single purchase.** Removing one purchase moves the target's rank by 1.3–1.5 positions on average under fU vs 3.1–3.2 under the dot (hm); only 12–13 % of fU hits have a purchase whose removal breaks the hit, vs 28–30 % for the dot. Under the cosine the mean is read as a *direction*, so single rows matter less — the same mechanism that flattens fU's HR across |H| in §2.4. The shopper-facing counterfactual sentence ("without your X …") is therefore *available for fewer users* under the layer than under the dot — a legibility cost of the layer to record, not a bug.
4. **~40–48 % of a user's purchases carry negative Shapley value toward the test positive** in every arm and on both datasets. "Your history supports this" is at best half true per purchase; any two-part honesty sentence (survey rule) has to allow negative evidence.
5. **ID share of attributed mass is tiny** (≤ 2.2 %) under the linear-share definition — different from the dc06 id_row_probe's energy-share definition; do not mix the two. On hm the fU ID row is inert (matches §2.4).

Caveats: single seed; the sampled-negatives protocol; ml-1m coverage stops at |H| ≤ 128 (24 % of the sample skipped); permutation-sampling error on ml-1m for the cosine arms; the hm fU / lightfm_hist checkpoints pre-date LOO (attribution geometry, not the accuracy rows, is what is read here — the plan's own eval-path argument).
