# Citable pattern: PPMs are the flagship of built-in-interpretability — anchor to their prominence, then distinguish ProtoMF

**The recurring literature pattern (worth citing):** across the intrinsic-XAI literature, **part-prototype models (PPMs, the ProtoPNet family) are repeatedly framed as the most prominent / canonical example of models with *built-in* interpretability** — the concrete flagship for Rudin's "use intrinsically interpretable models instead of post-hoc" agenda (S5 abstract: *"among which part-prototype models are particularly prominent"*; S5 §1; Rudin S1/C3). This is a ready-made **related-work anchor**: the "this looks like that" prototype-similarity principle is a recognized, well-cited pillar of interpretable ML.

**The rhetorical move for us:** ProtoMF is a **recsys instance of that same prototype-similarity principle** — so we get to inherit the PPM family's credibility and its stated open challenges (Axis-A semantic gaps) as motivation, *without* claiming to be a PPM. ProtoMF is **not a part-prototype model** (whole-object anchor directions, not spatially-decomposed object parts; ranking readout, not classification) — the mechanics of the distinction are in [[2026-06-16_2057_protomf-vs-ppm-positioning]]. Clean framing: "PPMs are the prominent intrinsic-interpretability exemplar; ProtoMF carries that principle into recommendation — sharing the principle *and* its challenges, differing in modality and readout."
