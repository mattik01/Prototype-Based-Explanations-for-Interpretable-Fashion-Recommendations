# Are we deliberately giving up a lot of predictive power? — the honest trade, and why sparsity flips its sign

*(Verbatim-capture entry — worked through interactively as a follow-up to [[2026-07-12_1200_linearity-interpretability-additive-attribution]]. The user flags this for **the Rashomon chapter** as the reviewer-inoculation / "does interpretability cost accuracy?" argument. Source material for a thesis passage — the passage itself is not yet written. Links to the constraint-helps note [[2026-06-11_1555_interpretability-constraints-as-expert-knowledge-infusion]] and the Rashomon positioning note [[2026-06-11_1722_rashomon-set-recsys-positioning]].)*

**The worry, stated sharply (user):** isn't the whole power of ML — especially neural nets — the fact that they leverage nonlinearity? By imposing linearity/additivity, aren't we deliberately giving up *a lot* of predictive power, handicapping a model that might function better with more mathematical freedom?

**The honest one-word answer: yes — in principle we are.** A constrained model can only be as good as, or worse than, the unconstrained one *on the training objective*. Pretending there's no trade would be dishonest, and it's the exact trade Rudin's stance is built on admitting: accept a possibly-small accuracy cost to buy interpretability. So the concession is real. **The entire argument is about the word "a lot"** — which is an assumption, not a fact — and here are the reasons it is probably wrong *for this project specifically*, followed by the honest scenario where it's right.

## Why "a lot" is probably wrong here (four reasons, plain build-up)

1. **"More mathematical freedom" is only an advantage when you have the data to spend it.** This is the pivot, and it's a statistical fact, not rhetoric. Extra nonlinear capacity = extra parameters describing feature *interactions*; to estimate those reliably you need data covering the relevant combinations. **H&M is definingly sparse** (~0.06% density, and we deliberately chose splits that *preserve* sparsity rather than sample it away). In that regime, more freedom mostly gives the model more ways to fit noise, popularity artifacts, and spurious co-occurrences it can't distinguish from signal. The freedom you "give up" is largely freedom you could never have cashed: the good function *exists* in the bigger hypothesis class, but SGD on a sparse dataset won't *find* it — it'll find a memorizing one. **Realized accuracy is bounded by data, not by capacity, once capacity exceeds what the data can pin down.** Recommendation is firmly in that data-bounded regime.

2. **So the constraint may be *buying* accuracy, not only spending it.** An architectural constraint is an inductive bias, and a bias that matches the true structure improves generalization. If the real signal is close to "user taste × item content, summed over features" — exactly what the bilinear-additive form encodes — then forbidding arbitrary within-side interactions is regularization. **On sparse data the sign of the trade often flips:** the interpretable model isn't handicapped, it's the better-conditioned estimator. Cold-start is the cleanest case — the additive form (user = Σ history rows + Σ attribute rows) degrades gracefully where a tangled model has nothing to fall back on, and there we expect it to *win* on accuracy outright, not merely tie. (Same pattern as the expert-knowledge-infusion note: the interpretable sleep-scoring model performed *better* because the expert vocabulary guided learning instead of forcing rediscovery — [[2026-06-11_1555_interpretability-constraints-as-expert-knowledge-infusion]].)

3. **There is no assumption-free "maximally free" model to be disadvantaged against.** Every architecture imposes priors: a CNN imposes translation equivariance, a transformer imposes attention structure, MF imposes bilinearity. "More freedom" is never free — it always trades against sample efficiency and identifiability. So we are **not** taking a maximally-powerful model and crippling it; we are *choosing a prior*, exactly as every architecture does, and choosing the one that matches both the data regime (sparse, categorical) and the goal (attribution). The real comparison is never "us vs. an unconstrained ideal" — it's "our prior vs. a *different* prior," and on this data ours is well-matched.

4. **We keep the nonlinearity that actually carries the signal.** The dominant nonlinearity in recommendation is the user×item cross, and the dot product retains it in full (it *is* a product — bilinear, not linear). What we forbid is *additional* entanglement *within* each side, which on categorical CF features tends to carry the least unique signal per parameter. So we don't give up the productive nonlinearity; we give up the one that mostly buys entanglement. (Precise restatement of the linearity entry: we rely on additivity *within* each side + a bilinear cross *between* sides, not on linearity everywhere — [[2026-07-12_1200_linearity-interpretability-additive-attribution]].)

## The honest other side — where the worry is right
If the H&M signal genuinely lives in rich higher-order feature *combinations* — "this brand × this season × this price tier" carvings that only a deep model can express *and* that we have enough data to estimate — then yes, we leave real accuracy on the table, and the additive form would show a measurable gap. That is a live empirical possibility, not something to argue away from the armchair. Two things keep it from being fatal: (a) if a *specific* interaction proves important, we can bring it back as an **upstream named module** without abandoning the additive combination (the CNN pattern generalizes — a learned "brand×season" feature becomes one more named row); (b) the thesis's contribution is the interpretability frontier, so a measured, honestly-reported gap is a *result*, not a failure.

## The discipline: we don't assert the cost is small — we instrument it
The like-for-like **host comparisons** (fI vs I-ProtoMF, fU vs U-ProtoMF, fUfI vs UI-ProtoMF) exist precisely to put a number on how much predictive power the grounding costs. The right way to state the whole thing in the thesis: **"interpretability gained per unit of accuracy conceded"** — with the denominator *measured* on H&M, assumed neither zero nor large.

## Candidate thesis framing (Rashomon chapter)
> Are we conceding predictive freedom? Yes. Are we conceding *a lot*? That is the empirical claim the worry points at — and two structural reasons say no *here*: (i) extreme sparsity is the regime where extra freedom hurts more than it helps, and (ii) we retain the one interaction that carries the signal (user×item) while dropping mostly the entangling kind. If those hold, the concession is small-to-negative; if they don't, the host comparisons will show it and we say so.

This is the natural companion to the Rashomon-set positioning ([[2026-06-11_1722_rashomon-set-recsys-positioning]]): a large recsys Rashomon set is *why* interpretability can be near-free; this entry supplies the mechanism — on sparse categorical interaction data, the interpretable prior sits comfortably inside that set and may even be the better estimator. **Caution carried over:** the Rashomon set can't be measured, so keep it as a conceptual lens; the accuracy-cost claim rests on the *measured* host gaps, not on the Rashomon construct.
