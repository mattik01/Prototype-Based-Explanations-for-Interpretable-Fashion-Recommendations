# Side note: interpretability constraints as expert-knowledge infusion channels

For the interpretable-ML-advantages thesis section: interpretability constraints double as channels for **infusing domain-expert knowledge** — constraining the model to reason in vocabulary experts already know to be effective. Example: interpretable sleep stage scoring (Niknazar & Mednick; my poster, now at `Master/literature/papers/C_prototype_interpretability/C6_niknazar_mednick_2025_interpretable_sleep_scoring_poster.pdf`) — the expert-constrained model became usable/trustable as a *tool* for domain experts (not a replacement) **and performed better**, which is plausible: the expert vocabulary is already proven for the problem, so the constraint guides learning instead of forcing the model to rediscover structure unconstrained. Connects to the earlier entries on interpretability penalties (regularizers) and interpretability ≠ less accuracy (Rashomon GC#9); same pattern as our feature-grounded prototypes, which infuse the fashion domain's vocabulary into ProtoMF.
