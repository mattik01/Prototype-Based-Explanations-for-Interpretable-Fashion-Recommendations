# Citable (S2 §3.8, p54): how deployed systems actually explain recommendations — post-hoc templates over statistics, decoupled from the model

Citation note from the S2 read (Zhang & Chen 2020, FnTIR — cite the published version, PDF is the arXiv twin). S2 §3.8 (p54) describes standard industry practice: items are recommended by "very sophisticated hybrid models," and the displayed explanation is generated *afterwards* from pre-defined templates filled with simple statistics ("70% of your friends bought this item"), selected by post-hoc criteria like maximum confidence — i.e. **the explanation channel is fully decoupled from the scoring mechanism**. Companion sentence on the same page for fairness when citing: post-hoc does not mean *fake* — the statistics are true information, just decoupled from the model.

**Use in the thesis:** the empirical anchor for "what explanation looks like in production" when motivating intrinsic explanations — our contrast is not against a strawman but against documented practice: true-but-decoupled template explanations vs a read-out that *is* the score decomposition ([[2026-07-14_1504_fidelity-term-disambiguation-relocated-not-escaped]]).
